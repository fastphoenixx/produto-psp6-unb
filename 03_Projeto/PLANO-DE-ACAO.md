### Recommended six-slide structure

1. **Problem and refined scope** — logistics/internal transport of sanitary ware; no solution.
2. **How evidence was gathered** — construction-site contact/interviews, distributor visit, photos, and desk research.
3. **Observed process** — supplier/distributor → delivery → storage → internal transport → installation.
4. **Evidence and findings** — where breakage was reported, contributing conditions, current controls, and consequences.
5. **What changed after fieldwork** — why the scope moved from generic handling/installation to transport/logistics.
6. **Gaps and next steps** — data still needed before the August 31 presentation.

## Evidence integrity

- Attribute the distributor visit to Rodrigo and identify it as a distributor/repair/logistics environment, not a construction site.
- Attribute construction-site photos and interviews to the person or group that collected them. Do not state that the whole team visited if it did not.
- Do not fabricate interview answers, visit details, breakage rates, injuries, or accident histories.
- Label conclusions as **observed**, **reported by interviewee**, **secondary data**, or **hypothesis to validate**.
- The current photos establish context but do not visibly prove broken sanitary ware or injury. Stronger close-up evidence is still needed for the August 31 presentation.
