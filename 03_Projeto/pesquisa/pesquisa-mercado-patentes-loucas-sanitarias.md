# Market and prior-art research — sanitary-ware transport breakage

## Purpose and scope

This brief supports the problem-sustantiation phase of the PSP6 project. The scope is ceramic sanitary ware—especially toilet bowls—from shipping/distribution to receipt and package opening at the construction site. It does not propose or select a solution.

The field evidence gathered by the team indicates a specific situation to investigate: a fixture may arrive cracked or broken inside packaging that appears intact, exposing the person who opens and inspects it to sharp ceramic edges.

## Executive finding

The most defensible market datum for the presentation is the scale of the Brazilian sector: **Brazil currently produces 22 million sanitary-ware pieces per year**, has **26 medium- and large-scale production units in eight states**, is among the **five largest producers worldwide**, and the segment supports approximately **7,000 direct jobs**. The source is ANFACER, the Brazilian industry association.

The preliminary patent scan confirms that transport breakage is a recognized technical problem. The closest documents concentrate on **cushioning, immobilization, package geometry, material reduction, and shipping density**. Among the documents reviewed, none directly addresses **warning the recipient that a ceramic fixture may be broken before the person reaches into the package** or **containing sharp fragments during safe inspection**. This is a preliminary opportunity signal, not proof of novelty or freedom to operate.

### What the datum does—and does not—prove

The 22 million figure proves **market scale**, not the frequency of transport breakage. It should be connected to the project as follows:

> “Brazil produces 22 million sanitary-ware pieces per year. This scale means that even a problem occurring in a small fraction of shipments may create repeated exposure during receipt and inspection. Our fieldwork identified the critical interaction: opening apparently intact packages containing already-damaged ceramic pieces.”

Do **not** convert the production total into an estimated number of broken pieces without a documented breakage rate. The desk research did not find an authoritative Brazilian rate specific to sanitary-ware transport.

## 2. Preliminary patent landscape

### Closest documents found

| Publication | Priority / publication | Main technical focus | Relevance and limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [US20150014208A1 / US9221577B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20150014208A1/en) — *Packaging system for toilet components* | Priority 2013; published 2015; granted; listed active | Corrugated-cardboard fittings arrange bowl, tank, and lid to protect fragile porcelain while reducing package volume. The specification claims about **27% less package volume** than conventional arrangements. | Strong evidence that breakage protection and logistics cost are recognized design drivers. It prevents damage; it does not alert or protect a worker from concealed breakage when opening. |
| [JP2007186235A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2007186235A/en) — *Packaging material for toilet bowl* | Priority 2006; published 2007 | Molded shock absorbers fixed inside a cardboard outer box; includes visual confirmation holes and easier assembly. | Relevant to cushioning and partial visibility. The inspection openings described are for viewing the accessory compartment, not detecting cracks or isolating sharp fragments. |
| [CN114194553A / CN114194553B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN114194553A/en) — *Packaging structure for toilet transportation* | Priority 2021; published 2022; grant reported in 2025 | Folded liner structure intended to replace wood/EPS, reduce cost and assembly difficulty, and protect against pressure, vibration, and drops. | Recent evidence that toilet transport packaging remains an active development area. Focus is transport protection and sustainability, not safe opening after damage. |
| [US10301097B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10301097B2/en) — *Shipping container for packaging frangible products* | Granted 2019 | Corrugated internal protective structure for large fragile products including toilets, sinks, and bathtubs. | Broad adjacent prior art on cushioning bulky fragile fixtures; does not target concealed-damage indication or safe inspection. |
| [USD795704S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD795704S1/en) — *Packaging for sanitary ware* | Priority 2016; granted 2017; listed active | Ornamental design for packaging that receives a toilet. | Confirms design-patent activity around sanitary-ware packaging, but its single claim is ornamental rather than a functional safety mechanism. |

Patent status labels above are database-reported and must be confirmed in the relevant patent office before any legal or freedom-to-operate decision.

### Technology clusters observed

1. **Impact attenuation:** molded buffers, corrugated inserts, honeycomb structures, EPS, or other cushioning.
2. **Immobilization and load distribution:** fittings that constrain the bowl, tank, or lid and reduce contact between ceramic parts.
3. **Logistics efficiency:** smaller package volume, more units per truck/container, and lower material or freight cost.
4. **Sustainability and assembly:** replacing wood/EPS and simplifying package assembly/disassembly.
5. **Package appearance:** ornamental configurations protected by design patents.

### Preliminary gap worth investigating

The first scan did not identify a sanitary-ware-specific document centered on the **worker’s interaction with a package that already contains concealed sharp fragments**. The next search should therefore expand beyond “toilet packaging” into adjacent patent classes and industries:

- irreversible shock/impact indicators attached to packages;
- damage-evident or tamper-evident packaging;
- safe-opening systems for glass, mirrors, stone slabs, and other brittle products;
- fragment-retention liners or bags;
- non-destructive crack detection during receiving inspection;
- cut-resistant access sleeves, flaps, or inspection windows.

Useful patent classification starting points from the closest US document are **B65D 5/50** (internal supporting or protecting elements) and its paper-element subgroups. Searches should combine these with terms such as `frangible`, `ceramic`, `sanitary ware`, `toilet bowl`, `damage indicator`, `shock indicator`, `fragment containment`, `safe opening`, and Portuguese equivalents.

## 3. Implications for the problem statement

The research supports three claims at different evidence levels:

| Claim | Evidence level | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| Sanitary ware is a large Brazilian product category. | Verified secondary evidence | “Brazil produces 22 million pieces annually, according to ANFACER.” |
| Transport protection is an established technical challenge. | Verified by multiple patent documents | “Multiple patent families address cushioning and package geometry for fragile ceramic fixtures.” |
| Existing solutions leave the package-opening risk unresolved. | Preliminary inference from a limited scan + team field evidence | “In the documents reviewed so far, we did not find a mechanism specifically aimed at protecting the recipient when concealed breakage is discovered during opening.” |

The third statement must remain qualified. Do not say “there are no patents” or “the market has no solution.”

## 4. Slide-ready content

### Recommended market slide

**Title:** A large logistics flow for a fragile product

**Main number:** 22 million sanitary-ware pieces produced per year in Brazil

**Supporting line:** 26 medium- and large-scale factories in eight states; Brazil is among the world’s five largest producers.

**Connection to the field evidence:** The team’s interviews indicate that some damage remains hidden until receipt and package opening—the moment in which the worker comes into contact with sharp ceramic edges.

**Footer:** Source: ANFACER, “Dados do setor — Louças sanitárias,” accessed 23 Aug. 2026.

### Recommended prior-art slide

**Title:** Prior art focuses on preventing breakage—not on safe discovery of breakage

- United States: compact corrugated fittings and protected positioning; claimed 27% package-volume reduction.
- Japan: molded shock absorbers integrated with a cardboard box.
- China: folded transport structure designed against pressure, vibration, and drops.
- Preliminary gap: no reviewed document specifically warns the handler before contact with concealed fragments or contains fragments during inspection.

**Footer:** Preliminary patent scan; not a novelty or freedom-to-operate opinion.

### 25-second speaking script

> “This is a large-scale flow: ANFACER reports annual Brazilian production of 22 million sanitary-ware pieces across 26 industrial units. Patent documents from the United States, Japan, and China show that manufacturers recognize transport breakage as a technical problem and invest mainly in cushioning, immobilization, and package efficiency. Our preliminary gap is different: in the documents reviewed, we did not find a mechanism focused on protecting the worker when a damaged piece remains hidden until the package is opened. That is exactly the interaction reported in our fieldwork.”

## 5. Research limits and next validation

- No authoritative Brazilian transport-breakage percentage was found. Any percentage supplied by a distributor, manufacturer, or construction site should identify the period, denominator, product category, and logistics stage.
- Patent searching is sensitive to terminology and classification. This first scan is sufficient to orient the academic presentation, but not to establish novelty.
- The strongest next primary datum would be: `damaged units / total units received` for a defined project or distributor and time window.
- The next field questions should distinguish manufacturing defect, line-haul transport, unloading, storage, and internal handling; “arrived broken” alone does not identify the causal stage.
- The opportunity statement should continue to focus on the worker and the opening/inspection event until new evidence justifies changing it.
