## Evidence status legend

- **Confirmed:** supported by an original recording, direct observation, identifiable interview, document, or authoritative source.
- **Reported:** stated by a participant or contact, but the original evidence has not yet been archived.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible interpretation still requiring field validation.
- **Drafted:** text produced for a report or presentation that must not be treated as evidence by itself.
- **Rejected:** contradicted or deliberately removed from the project narrative.

## Weekly record

### Week 01 — 2026-08-10 to 2026-08-16

#### What happened

- The team was formed with Arthur, Igor, Larissa, Pedro, and Rodrigo.
- The class theme was understood as ESG–Social, occupational health and safety in construction.
- Several possible problems were discussed, including silica exposure and sanitary-ware logistics.
- The group selected sanitary-ware breakage/handling as the initial direction.
- Larissa prepared the first report and presentation drafts using the Double Diamond and Stakeholder Mapping.

#### Result

- Initial broad problem: breakage of sanitary ware creates sharp fragments, possible cuts, material loss, delay, and replacement cost.
- No direct site visit had occurred by the end of the week.

#### Lesson

- The topic was attractive but still combined several distinct problems and several stages of the process.

### Week 02 — 2026-08-17 to 2026-08-23

#### What happened

- The team presented the initial problem on August 17.
- During the presentation, the team explicitly stated that it had not yet visited a construction site and that field validation was pending.
- The professor corrected the interpretation of the stakeholder matrix.
- The professor required the team to choose a specific construction scenario.
- The professor instructed the team to investigate where, when, why, and how breakage occurs, directly with the operator and through context observation.
- Rodrigo visited a distributor/repair/logistics operation and reported that breakage is concentrated in logistics.
- Construction-site photos and information were received through another group/contact.
- Larissa produced a completed-visit draft and a consolidated report draft.

#### Confirmed evidence

- Audio of the August 17 presentation and professor feedback.
- Rodrigo's distributor/warehouse photos.
- Construction-site context photos received through another contact.
- Professor's acceptance of transport or packaging as possible directions if evidence supports them.

#### Reported but not yet archived as primary evidence

- Distributor statement that breakage occurs mainly in logistics.
- Details of who Rodrigo interviewed and the exact operational flow observed.
- Any direct worker testimony from the construction site.

#### Drafted but not yet verified

- The Noroeste site characterization in Larissa's field report.
- Four detailed interview transcripts.
- Two cutting incidents and internal clinic records.
- SESMT's alleged confirmation of underreporting.
- Precise observations about PPE, fragments, disposal, and return procedures.

#### Professor's requests carried into the next week

- Correct the influence×interest matrix.
- Define the construction scenario and project boundary.
- Show traceable sources.
- Interview/observe the operator.
- Establish the exact point, moment, cause, and consequences of breakage.
- Decide from evidence whether the project is about transport, packaging, receipt/opening, internal movement, or installation.

#### Current unresolved questions

1. Which of Larissa's field claims are backed by original notes, audio, photos, or identifiable interviewees?
2. What exactly did Rodrigo observe and hear at the distributor?
3. Does the dominant breakage happen before arrival, during receiving/opening, during internal transport, or during installation?
4. Is the primary worker harm a cutting hazard, ergonomic overload, or another mechanism?
5. Is the primary context a large developer-led residential project, a smaller private project, or the distribution chain?
6. Who has the purchasing/adoption decision in the selected context?

#### Evidence collected — 2026-08-24

- **Confirmed:** short recorded interview with Lucas, a construction worker active since 2009. He reported having handled sanitary ware, witnessed pieces slip and fall during truck unloading, encountered pieces already cracked, and manually carried pieces where carts could not access.
- **Confirmed limit:** Lucas did not witness a worker being injured by breakage. The interview does not quantify occurrence, loss, or the dominant causal stage.
- **New hypothesis strengthened:** damage may be present before installation and may occur during unloading; internal manual movement is an additional exposure point.
- Transcript and methodological reading saved at `campo/entrevista-2026-08-24/Transcricao_Suprema_Lucas.md`.
